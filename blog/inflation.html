Inflation is theft contrived by the powerful

No war but the class war. We are living in a monopoly society, where the government and corporations exploit us out of greed.

Everything we consume carries a tax from the government and a markup from the corporations. As a result, their greed keeps growing until it eats itself, like an ouroboros.

I am not a communist. Rather, I am an anarchist, or a libertarian, so I distrust the government and the corporations, which are in cahoots behind the scenes like a cartel. Our ideologies differ, whether communist, anarchist, or libertarian, but we reach the same conclusion: inflation is theft, and we need to end centralized authority.

Inflation is a redistribution from the poor to the powerful: a regressive tax on the poor.

Almost every country has the same policy of inflating the currency at a rate of 2% per year to promote the economic growth they believe in. However, regardless of economic advances, inflation devastates the lives of the poor and makes those in power richer.

Historically, inflation, aka printing money, was counterfeiting: the value of money was diluted by mixing gold or silver with cheap metal. Through this process, the king could flourish for a long time. In contrast, modern inflation is not obvious to everyone. Governments and central banks have invented a genius method to hide the debasement of their currency.

So why do they do it? Because they gain from it. The government is the biggest debtor, and inflation helps it pay its debts without people noticing that it is a hidden tax hike. Moreover, I believe one of the main roles of the government is to exploit people as much as possible to maintain vested interests. Therefore, it has every reason to keep inflation going and to make it look normal.

For example, they successfully gaslight stupid citizens into believing that deflation is “bad” for the country, and that to curb deflation we need inflation at a constant pace every year. In addition, people tend to believe inflation always comes with a rise in their salary. As a result, they have the delusion that their salaries have actually gone up. But at the same time, inflation steals their income and savings, and they don’t notice.

Furthermore, the burden is not equal. The powerful own assets that rise with prices, whereas the poor hold cash and savings that lose value. That is why inflation is a regressive tax. Once inflation is accepted by the public, it keeps people working much later in their careers because they can’t afford to live off their deteriorating pensions. Besides, it pushes them to invest in the rigged stock market to satisfy the greed of corporations.

Crises make it even worse. I suspect that corporations use war, natural disasters, and pandemics as excuses to hike prices. We remember that wheat prices soared during the Russia–Ukraine war, oil prices spiked during the US–Iran war, and the price of nearly everything surged during Covid. Once prices go up, they rarely come back down. We get used to the higher prices and accept them as the new normal. Through these crises, inflation often becomes uncontrollable. Then the central bank decides to raise the policy rate to curb the “excess inflation,” because it believes in the 2% goddess. But in my view, the rate hike only makes the powerful who own bonds richer.

In short, it is a cycle: inflate, then tighten, then inflate again. Ordinary people are caught in the swing, while corporations, capital, and the government stay on the winning side. This is the rigged pendulum we live in.

Moreover, inflation is dangerous in itself. Easy money inflates economic bubbles and causes unnecessary boom-and-bust cycles, as in the run-up to the Great Depression of 1929. In the extreme, when a government finances itself by printing money, we get hyperinflation like Germany in 1923. In such turbulence, legal tender can turn into a mere piece of paper, and many of us could go hungry. Potatoes and carrots would be more valuable than money.

Unfortunately, it is almost impossible to dismantle the authority, which means inflation will keep going. Nevertheless, if we learn from history, we can, as individuals, avoid this rigged society and run away from it. In Russia, for instance, the dacha has helped families get through times of food shortage. Likewise, during the German hyperinflation, food was worth far more than paper money, and people bartered for it. Therefore, we need to build small communities that can survive by being self-sufficient in basic food and a water source.
