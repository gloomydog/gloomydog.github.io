NAAIM Exposure Index vs. Stock Indices

The NAAIM Exposure Index shows the average exposure to U.S. equities reported each week by members of the National Association of Active Investment Managers (NAAIM). It tracks how much professional money managers actually have invested in stocks. Readings below 0 mean they are net short, and readings above 100 mean they are using leverage. Many investors watch it as a sentiment gauge: very high readings often signal complacency, while very low readings tend to show up near market bottoms.

NAAIM used to publish the index freely, but it now restricts access, so the figures available to the public lag by about three weeks. I post each new reading here as soon as it comes out on Wednesday and chart it alongside the major U.S. stock indices, so you can compare the latest sentiment with price at a glance.

If you want to see the raw numbers behind this chart, they’re available on my GitHub page.
